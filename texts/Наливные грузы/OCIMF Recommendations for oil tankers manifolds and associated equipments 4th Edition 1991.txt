Introduction

The Oil Companies International Marine Forum first published “Standards for Oil Tanker Manifolds and
Associated Equipment” in 1971. The guidance was subsequently revised in a Second Edition, published in
1975, primarily to take account of changes in practice reflecting the increased usage of hard arms, the
introduction of large diameter floating hoses at some SBM’'s and the increased frequency of lightening
operations.

A Third Edition, published in 1981, contained revised and expanded recommendations relating to lifting
equipment.

In this Fourth Edition, no substantive changes have been made to the previously-published guidance relating to
cargo and bunker manifolds and associated equipment. However, in order to take account of the requirement at
a limited number of terminals for tankers in certain trades to transfer cargo vapours ashore, a new Annex has
been included containing recommendations for vapour manifolds. It is stressed that these Annexed
recommendations are provided purely for the guidance of those operators whose ships may trade to terminals
required to install vapour collection systems. The recommendations supplement the uniform safety design
standards for marine vapour recovery systems being developed by the International Maritime Organization
(IMO) and the regulations for marine vapour control systems promulgated by the United States Coast Guard in
1990. The recommendations relate only to the provision of a uniform manifold arrangement and do not
address other issues associated with vapour transfer systems such as the need to provide safeguards to prevent
or control vapour release or to prevent fire and explosion.

2. MANIFOLD POSITION,

2.1
Centre of Cargo Manifold

The centre of the cargo manifold arrangement should be located at the mid-length of the ship, or as near as
possible, but in no case should it be more than 3.0m forward or aft of the mid-length. For these purposes, the
ship’s length should be taken as the length overall.

2.2
Distance from Ship’s Side

The distance of the presentation flanges inboard from the ship’s side should be 4.6m.

2.3
Height of Cargo Manifold
2.3.1. The centres of the manifold presentation flanges should be located at least 700mm above the horizontal
projection of the top of the hose support at the ship’s side. (See Section 8).

2.3.2 The height of the centres of the presentation flanges above the deck should not exceed 2.1m.

2.3.3 The working platform should be fitted to allow 900mm between the level of the platform and the centres
of the presentation flanges. (See Section 3.)

2.4
Spacing

The minimum spacing of the cargo manifold as measured centre to centre along the line of presentation flanges
should be in accordance with Table 1 below.

3. SPILL TANK AND WORKING PLATFORM

All ships should be provided with permanently fitted working platforms and spill tanks under the cargo and
bunker manifold connections.

3.1
Size and Position

Length — The spill tank and the working platform should extend beyond the bunker connections at the forward
and aft ends on the manifold. On ships provided with vapour return facilities (see Annex), the spill tank and the
working platform should extend beyond the vapour manifold connections.

Width — The spill tank and working platform should have a width of approximately 1.8m and should be so
positioned that about 1.2m is outboard of the reducer presentation flanges.

Depth — The spill tank should have a minimum depth of about 300mm.

Vertical Positioning — The working platform should be situated 900mm below the level of the centres of the
reducer presentation flanges and the spill tank should be located below the working platform.

3.2
Spill Tank Draining

Suitable means of draining the spill tank should be provided. When considering draining facilities, the
particular type of tanker, its trading pattern and the cargoes likely to be carried, should be taken into account.

3.3
Strength of Working Platform

The strength and construction of the working platform and supports should be such that all normal loads
encountered during hose connection, cargo handling and disconnection cau be adequately catered for. The grids
forming the working surface should be capable of withstanding a load of at least 1 tonne/m?. (See also Section 4.4).

3.4
Design of Working Platform
The working platform surface should be made from open mesh grating to allow free passage of any oil spilt

from the manifold into the spill tank.

The grid should have a non-slip finish and should be removable, in easily handled sections, to permit access to
the spill tank.

The outboard uppermost edge of the working platform/spill tank should be fitted with a 50mm radius rounded
edge to protect cargo and bunker hoses during connection and disconnection.

3.5
Access to Working Platform

A Steel stairway of suitable size should be provided at the forward and aftermost ends of the platform leading
to the deck.

3.6

Combined Working Platforms and Spill Tanks

Where the working platform and spill tank have been designed as a combined unit, the dimensions and strength
should conform with those outlined in 3.1 and 3.3. In addition, the other provisions in Section 3 should be followed
as closely as possible, including those regarding the provision of hose protection on leading edges.

5. CARGO MANIFOLD SPECIFICATION AND FITTINGS

5.1

Valves
5.1.1 Material
The cargo manifold valves should be made of steel and be fitted with flanges conforming preferably to B.S.
1560 or ANSI B16.5.
5.1.2 Actuators
Cargo manifold valve actuators, when fitted, should be placed on the inboard side of the valve to avoid damage
and overcrowding during hose handling operations.
5.2
Distance Pieces
5.2.1 Material

Steel distance pieces with outboard flanges conforming to B.S.1560 or ANSI B16.5 should be fitted outboard of
the manifold valves and immediately inboard of the reducer. The inboard flanges should preferably conform to
B.S. 1560 or ANSI В16.5.

5.2.2 Length

The length of the distance pieces should be kept to a minimum. However, their length should be adequate to
provide sufficient room for the following:-

i) To ensure that sufficient space is available for bolt withdrawal from the valve/distance piece and distance
piece/reducer flanges. A minimum of 200mm should be allowed between each flange and the manifold
support structure.

ii) To accommodate the manifold support.

iii) To accommodate other line connections including stripping discharges and drain lines which may be
required by owners. Such lines, when incorporated, should be fitted inboard of the manifold supports.
5.3
Reducers
5.3.1 Material
All reducers should be made of steel and fitted with flanges that conform to B.S. 1560 or ANSI В16.5. The inboard
flange should be made to bolt directly to the distance piece (see Section 5.2).

The outboard flange on all reducers should be known as the “presentation flange”.

5.3.2 Length

All reducers should have an overall length of 500mm in order to allow sufficient space for the operation of
quick acting couplers.

5.3.3 Lifting Lugs

All reducers should be fitted with a lifting lug. This lug should be placed as near to tne centre of balance as
possible and at a location which should not interfere with either the operation of quick acting couplers or with
the bolting up of flanges.

5.3.5 Material and Design

6. DECK FITTINGS

This section covers the design of the deck fittings which should be provided for hoisting aud hanging off hoses
at sea berths.

6.1
Cruciform Bollards

Sets of cruciform bollards, 600mm in height, should be welded to the deck on each side of the ship. The
bollards should be fitted mid-way between each pair of cargo manifolds, allowing maximum clear area
between the working platform and ship’s side and yet giving sufficient room for safe access (about 300mm)
between bollard and spill tank. The working load of these bollards should be as detailed in Table 4.

6.2

Bollards (Bitts)

Two pairs of bollards, 300mm in diameter for tonnage category A, B and C ships and 350mm in diameter for
tonnage category D ships, should be provided on each side of the ship, one pair forward and one pair aft of the
manifold.

These bollards should be conveniently placed near to, but outside of, the deck area between the manifold and the
ship’s side and in the same longitudinal line as the cruciform bollards (see Section 6.1).

The sets of boards should be aligned and positioned as shown in Figures 1-4 and the working load should
conform to Table 4.

6.3
Fairleads

Fairleads with clear openings 400 x 250 mm should be provided at the ship’s side for hose chains and hose
hoisting as detailed below:

a) For ship tonnage categories A and B - two fairleads having a working load of 25 tonnes should be
provided on each side directly in line with the forward and aftermost cargo presentation flanges.

b) For ship tonnage categories C and D - fairleads having a working load of 40 tonnes should be
provided on each side directly in line with each cargo manifold presentation flange.

For ships in category D, a clear run should be provided along the ship’s deck to deck winches for
leading the hose hoisting wires which are occasionally used at some sea terminals.

6.4
Deck Rings or Lugs

Deck rings or lugs of 15 tonnes working load should be fitted on each side of the ship in the following
positions:-

i) On the deck adjacent to each bunker manifold and in the same longitudinal line as the cruciform
bollards (see Section 6.1)

ii) On the deck adjacent to the bollards described in 6.2 (see plans, Figures 1 - 4).
iii) On the deck adjacent to each fairlead described in 6:3:

iv) On the deck adjacent to the hose support at the ship’s side opposite each bunker connection.

7. LIGHTENING ARRANGEMENTS

Note: these recommendations are only applicable to ships within tonnage category D.
7.1
Fairleads

Two additional sets of heavy duty closed fairleads with clear openings of 500 x 400 mm should be positioned on
the starboard side of the ship. These fairleads should be located 35m forward and aft of the mid-length of the ship
or as close to this position as possible. All faitleads associated with ship to ship transfer operations should be
smoothly finished on both inboard and outboard faces to prevent chafing of ropes.

7.2
Bollards (Виз)

Twin sets of bollards of 550mm diameter should be located inboard of the fairteads (section 7.1) for handling the
moorings used during lightening operations.

7.3

Other Mooring Fairleads

All other fairleads used during mooring should be of the closed type to avoid difficulties occurring owing to the
large relative changes of freeboard experienced during lightening operations.

8. HOSE SUPPORT AT SHIP’S SIDE

Means should be provided to adequately support hoses in way of the ship’s side abreast of the manifolds.

8.1
Curved Plate or Pipe Hose Support

In order to protect the hose from sharp edges and obstructions. a horizontal curved plate or pipe section should
be fitted at the ship’s side and above the fairleads described in Section 6.3.

The curved plate or pipe section should be fitted with its rounded surface uppermost and in such a position that
the maximum surface area is presented to a hose coming on board from the sea.

The radius of curvature of this plate or pipe section should not be less than that quoted in Table 5 below.

8.2

Hose Support Position

The hose supports should lie in the fore and aft axis at the ship's sides and be at least 700mm below the level of
the centres of the presentation flanges. The hose support should extend beyond the centre of the bunker
connections or, if applicable, vapour connections (see Annex), at the forward and aft ends of the manifold.

8.3
Hose Support Strength

a) For ship tonnage categories A and B - hose supports should be sized to cater for a minimum vertical
load of two hoses each of 10 tonnes.

b) For ship tonnage category C - hose supports should be sized to cater for a minimum vertical load of
two hoses each of 20 tonnes.

с) For ship tonnage category D - hose supports should be sized to cater for a minimum vertical load of
two hoses each of 25 tonnes.

9.1
Number and Position

Four bunker connections should be provided, two on each side of the ship, with presentation flanges 4.6m from

the base of the cargo connections.

One bunker connection should be located forward of the manifold and one located aft of the manifold on each

10. LIGHTING

The levels of illumination in way of the manifold should not be less than 50 lux when measured on a parallel
with and 1m above the deck.

This illuminated area should extend overside to the waterline in light displacement in order to provide
illumination for handling hoses at sea berths.

Levels of illumination over the rest of the working deck should have an average minimum lighting intensity of
10 lux when measured on a parallel with and 1m above the deck.

Area of Operation of Lifting Equipment
The lifting equipment should be able to achieve the appropriate SWL as shown in Table 9 when plumbing a point

11,3
Operating Criteria for Lifting Equipment

The lifting equipment fitted should be able to operate as follows:
® to top and lower under load and be fail-safe in the event of loss of power when topping or lowering;

@ to slew both ways by power (not manually) under load, both when topping and lowering and when not
topping and lowering;

e the hook and runner should run free without manual intervention under conditions of no load;

@ to lift and lower at a rate at the hook of between 10 and 15 metres per minute under load and
approximately 20 to 30 metres per minute under conditions of no load,

@ to operate smoothly during lifting or lowering to a fineness of control of a maximum of one inch at the
hook. The equipment should have a similar smoothness of operation when slewing. (This
recommendation is particularly important for safety of operations when matching hoses to the manifold,
fitting heavy reducing pieces, etc.);

© to be operated from a central control position for all functions - lifting and lowering, topping and

@ to present the hook or shackle on a single part of wire at the water’s edge.

on the ship, from a large block with several turns of wire close to them. As an interim measure, a
suitably-sized 3-metre pennant may be fitted under the block).
11.4

Other Criteria for Derrick Systems

All slewing operations should be powered by a single winch.

All operations-lifting and lowering, topping and lowering and slewing - should be carried out by a single winch
complex,

Conflict of Criteria

Requirements of the Flag State or International regulations may be in excess of the above recommendations and
should be observed in the event of conflicting criteria.

Recommendations for Vapour Manifolds

Note: The following recommendations are provided for the guidance of tanker operators whose ships may trade
to terminals where it is a requirement for cargo vapours to be transferred ashore.

A.1 GENERAL

This Annex only provides information relating to vapour manifolds that is additional to the recommendations
for cargo manifolds and associated equipment. Reference should therefore be made to the relevant section of
the text for cargo manifolds for details of tonnage categories, manifold position and materials.

A.2 MANIFOLD POSITION

А.2.1 Spacing

The minimum spacing of the vapour manifolds as measured from centre to centre from the bunker lines along
the line of presentation flanges should be in accordance with Table A.1 below.

TABLE A.1: MINIMUM VAPOUR MANIFOLD SPACING

Ship Tonnage Category Vapour Manifold Spacing
Centre to Centre
A 1.5 metres
BCD ' 2.0 metres

For retrofit installations aboard ships where significant obstructions prevent the location of the vapour manifolds
in accordance with Table A.1, the following alternative arrangements may be considered,

a) The vapour manifolds may be located fore and aft of the bunker lines at a lesser distance than that
prescribed in Table А.1. However, the distance from the bunker line should not be less than 1.0 metre.
b) Should no other arrangement be possible, the vapour manifolds may be located directly above the

centrelines of the existing bunker lines (‘piggy backed’). However, it should be noted that this
arrangement may restrict the ship’s capability to load bunkers concurrently with vapour recovery
operations. If an existing working platform is to be used with this arrangement, the height of the
centre of the vapour line’s presentation flange above the working platform should not exceed 1.5
metres.

A.2.2 Number and Position

Four vapour connections should be provided, two on each side of the ship, with presentation flanges at the same
height above the deck as the cargo manifold. One vapour connection should be located forward of the manifold
and one located aft of the manifold on each side of the ship.

A.2.3 Labelling

The first 1.0 metre inboard of each manifold connection should be painted on its exterior surfaces, excluding
flange faces. The painted area should be divided into three bands with the outboard and inboard bands being red
in colour and 100mm wide and the centre band being yellow in colour. In addition, the word ‘VAPOUR’ should
be painted in black letters at least 50mm high on both the forward and aft sides of all reducers, both principal and
reserve, in approximately the 2 o’clock and 10 o'clock positions. (see Figure A.6)

А.5.2.2. Material and Design
All presentation flanges should conform to ANSI B16.5. They should be kept vertical and should have plain (flat)
faces, i.e. raised faced flanges should not be used.

In order to prevent the possible misconnection of the vapour manifold to a liquid loading line, a mechanical
keying arrangement should be provided for the presentation flanges as follows (see also Figure A.5):

1) The bolt locations should be arranged so that two bolts straddle the 12 o’clock position at the top of the
flange face.
2) Onecylindrical stud should be permanently attached to each of the presentation flange faces at the 12 o’clock

position on the respective flange bolt circles. This cylindrical stud should project horizontally outboard
perpendicular to the flange face and be centred on the same bolt circle as defined in ANSI В16,5. Thediameter
of the cylindrical stud should be 12.7mm (0.5 inches) and it should be at least 25.4mm (1.0 inches) long.

А.5.2.3.. Types of Flanges

Flanges should conform to the recommendations contained in Section 5.3.6.

А.5.2.4 Blank Flanges

Each presentation flange of the principal reducers/expanders should be provided with a removable steel blank
flange fitted with handles. Each blank flange should be painted yellow on its exteriour surfaces (excluding the
inboard — facing flange face). To accommodate the lug on the vapour line presentation flange, each blank
flange should have an extra hole located in the 12 o’clock position on the flange’s bolt hole circle. The diameter
of the hole should be 15.88 mm (0.625 inches).
